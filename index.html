About

I am a Graduate Research and Teaching Assistant specializing in environmental photochemistry and bioenergy conversion. Currently pursuing my MS at North Dakota State University, my research focuses on herbicide photodegradation, radical kinetics, and predictive modeling (QSAR/MLR). I have previously worked on projects related to the hydrothermal liquefaction (HTL) of waste biomass into bio-oil and bio-asphalt, biohythane production, and conducting techno-economic analyses of sustainable fuels. With expertise in advanced analytical techniques such as HPLC-MS, GC-MS, FTIR, and SEM, alongside proficiency in ArcGIS, MATLAB, and Python for data modeling, I am committed to advancing sustainable solutions in environmental chemistry, bioenergy, and resource recovery.

Project

Herbicide leftover removal using safeners in simulated sunlight on solid surface

  • Designed and executed systematic photolysis experiments on a diverse matrix of 9 herbicides and 11 safeners using sunlight simulator, uncovering a previously unreported degradation pathway
  • Demonstrated universal enhancement of herbicide degradation by safeners, achieving up to a 10.45-fold acceleration in the photodegradation of the highly persistent herbicide atrazine
  • Identified the dominant photochemical mechanism as a catalytic photosensitization cycle, mediated by the safener’s triplet excited state (3S*) and singlet oxygen (1O2), confirmed through targeted chemical probe assays
  • Developed highly accurate QSAR models (R2 > 0.99), revealing low hydrogen-bonding capacity as the key molecular determinant of efficient photodegradation and photosensitization
  • Established a rational, chemistry-based design framework for dual-benefit agrochemical formulations, enabling effective weed management with reduced environmental persistence

Fate of Biocides and Phthalates on Photodegradation using UV 222 and UV 254 nm

  • Experimental design and ran controlled, surface, and aquous photodegradation experiments at 222 and 254 nm for selected 10 biocides and phthalates, and HPLC/LC for kinetics
  • At UV 222 nm, 2–10 times higher decay observed compared to UV 254 nm for 95% of the compounds

Predictive Modeling of Radical Reaction Kinetics via Multilinear Regression (MLR)

  • Compiled a literature-based dataset of rate constants (k) for four key radicals (OH•, NO2•, CO3•⁻, SO4•⁻) and calculated 12 molecular descriptors capturing structural and physicochemical properties
  • Built multilinear regression models using Python with strong predictive accuracy (R2 = 0.79–0.96) for OH•, NO2•, and CO3•⁻, while identifying limitations for SO4•⁻
  • Performed sensitivity analysis (SRCs), revealing bioavailability score and hydrophobicity (Log S, Log Kp) as dominant reactivity drivers
  • Outlined future improvements via machine learning (Random Forest, QM descriptors) to enhance predictive robustness and mechanistic insight

Biohythane Production Enhancement via Microalgae–Fecal Sludge Co-Digestion in Two-Stage Anaerobic Systems

  • Performed two-stage anaerobic digestion (TSAD) experiments using microalgae–fecal sludge co-substrates to optimize the C/N ratio for enhanced biohythane (bioH2 + bioCH4) production
  • Achieved a biohydrogen yield of 24.5 mL/g-VS and a biomethane yield of 281 mL/g-VS, with a 52.7% synergistic improvement over microalgae alone
  • Conducted techno-economic analysis, revealing an ROI of 4.8–5.8 and potential cost reduction up to 75.6% at optimized biomass ratios, highlighting economic feasibility
  • Demonstrated that microalgae–sludge co-digestion is a cost-effective, scalable strategy for sustainable biohythane production and waste-to-energy valorization

Low-Phenolic Naphtha-Rich Biocrude Production via Hydrothermal Liquefaction (HTL)

  • Optimized co-liquefaction of fecal sludge and organic solid waste under HTL (320 °C, 60 min), achieving a biocrude yield of 55.7% with 90% lighter fraction (85% naphtha, 5% jet fuel)
  • Characterized bio-crude via GC-MS, FTIR, and elemental analysis, confirming <1% phenolic content, 23.3 API gravity, and high heating value (37.5 MJ/kg) comparable to Petro crude
  • Demonstrated low phenolic toxicity due to replacement of –OH groups with stable ethoxy groups, enhancing naphtha selectivity
  • Performed energy balance and techno-economic assessment, showing energy recovery of 88%, energy consumption ratio of 0.38, and potential revenue of US$ 550/MT feedstock

Production of Performance-Grade Bio-Asphalt via Hydrothermal Liquefaction of Fecal Sludge with Natural Clay Catalysts in Pilot-Scale

  • Conducted pilot-scale hydrothermal liquefaction (HTL) of fecal sludge at 300 °C, 60 min using natural clay catalysts (kaolin, bentonite, dolomitic limestone, local clays)
  • Achieved a maximum biocrude yield of 54 wt% with 56 wt% asphaltene content using bentonite clay, enriched in hydrocarbons (61.5% fraction by GC-MS) with a heating value of 34.2 MJ/kg
  • Demonstrated catalytic enhancement by bentonite (14.7% Fe2O3), which promoted deoxygenation, hydrogenation, and decarboxylation reactions, yielding an asphaltene-rich product
  • Evaluated thermal stability (TGA) and rheological properties (G/sinδ), confirming classification as a high-temperature PG 76-grade binder with superior rutting resistance
  • Highlighted the commercial potential of bio-asphalt as a sustainable, low-cost alternative to petroleum-derived asphalt for high-temperature paving applications
